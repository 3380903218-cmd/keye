# HY6101 作业要求与老师设问

## 作业要求

> Each week, draft a book review/analysis overview of the article or book that you have read for the week. This essay should be both (1) a description and (2) analysis of the reading's conceptual framing, it's organization/argument, it's analysis/approach, its sources, its readability/writing style, and its contribution to how we think about texts, archives, sources, narratives, discourses, memory, or any combination of the those themes. If there are other themes, please also include. Aim for a length between 800 to 1000 words.

## 老师设问

> In groups of 3–5, discuss the following questions. Use specific ideas or examples from Elizabeth Yale’s article, and connect them where possible to your own research or experience working with historical sources.

### 1. Archives and neutrality

> Yale argues that archives are not neutral collections of evidence. What does this mean?
>
> Identify one example from the article that shows how archives are shaped by power, institutions, or decisions about what is preserved.

### 2. Document creation

> How does thinking about how a document was created change the way we read it?
>
> Choose a type of source you have used—such as a government report, court record, newspaper, interview, photograph, or letter. Who created it, for what purpose, and for whom?

### 3. Archival gaps and silences

> What can archival gaps or silences tell us?
>
> Think of a group, experience, or perspective that is difficult to find in the archive. Why might it be missing, and how might a historian respond to that absence?

### 4. Power and weakness

> Can archives reveal both power and weakness?
>
> Yale shows that archives may demonstrate state control, but also confusion, incomplete knowledge, and institutional anxiety. Can you think of an example from your own research where an official source shows both authority and uncertainty?

### 5. Future archival practice

> What would you now do differently when using an archive?
>
> Based on Yale’s article, identify one question you would ask about the archive itself before treating its documents as historical evidence.
