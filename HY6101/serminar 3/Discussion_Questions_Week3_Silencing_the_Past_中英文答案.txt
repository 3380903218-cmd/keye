问题一：第一章——沉默从哪里进入历史生产？
Question 1: Chapter 1—Where do silences enter the production of history?

中文答案

Trouillot 把历史理解为双重过程：一方面是过去实际发生的社会历史过程，另一方面是后来关于这些过程的知识、记忆和叙述。因此，历史不是过去事件的透明镜像，而是在不同阶段被生产出来的。他指出，沉默至少在四个相互连接的时刻进入历史生产：事实创造、事实汇编、事实检索和回顾性意义。

第一，事实创造是来源的形成。事件必须通过报告、法律文件、报纸、信件、口述、物质遗存或身体痕迹，才会成为能够被后人使用的“事实”。记录者的身份、语言、制度位置和目的，会影响什么被记录以及什么没有被记录。例如，殖民官员、军队、法院和种植园主往往比普通奴隶、妇女和乡村劳动者更有机会留下连续的书面记录。没有记录不等于没有行动，而是说明某些行动没有被当时的权力认可为值得记录的事实。

第二，事实汇编是档案的形成。来源需要经过收集、筛选、分类、编目、保存、转移和数字化，才会成为研究者可以接触的档案。档案不是自然形成的中立仓库，而是社会和制度的中介。档案机构会决定哪些文件被视为正式文件、哪些材料具有长期价值、哪些可以销毁，以及研究者应当如何检索它们。

第三，事实检索是叙事的形成。历史学家并不能接触过去的全部材料，而只能使用那些被保存、编目、开放并且自己能够理解的材料。研究者选择档案馆、语言、关键词、时间范围和证据标准时，也在进行筛选。于是，叙事的缺口既可能来自过去没有留下记录，也可能来自现在无法访问或没有检索到记录。

第四，回顾性意义是历史在“最后阶段”的形成。纪念日、博物馆、教科书、国家仪式和公共展览会重新决定某个事件是否重要、应该用什么名称纪念，以及它应当服务于怎样的身份和政治目的。10 月 12 日被称为“哥伦布日”还是土著人民日，就不只是日期问题，而是关于殖民、征服、民族和记忆的意义选择。

对历史学家而言，最困难的并不是发现某一份文件有偏见，而是辨认那些已经被档案制度和既有概念隐藏起来的缺席。四个阶段彼此相连：早期没有记录，会减少档案中的材料；档案分类又会影响后来检索和叙事；最后的纪念和教材还会让某种叙事看起来像自然事实。因此，我认为事实汇编和事实检索构成特别大的挑战，因为它们决定研究者实际上能够看到什么。不过，历史学家不能彻底消除沉默。能做的是让沉默可见，说明它如何形成，并通过不同类型、不同语言和不同立场的材料进行交叉验证。历史研究的目标不是假装获得完整过去，而是在承认限制的基础上产生更负责任的新叙事。

English answer

Trouillot understands history as a double process. It refers both to the sociohistorical process of what happened and to the later knowledge, memory, and narrative about what happened. History is therefore not a transparent mirror of the past. It is produced through several stages, and silence can enter at each stage: fact creation, fact assembly, fact retrieval, and retrospective significance.

Fact creation means the making of sources. An event becomes a usable historical fact only when it is recorded in a report, law, newspaper, letter, oral testimony, material object, or bodily trace. The recorder’s identity, language, institutional position, and purpose shape what is written down. Colonial officials, armies, courts, and plantation owners usually had more opportunities to leave continuous written records than enslaved people, women, or rural workers. The absence of a record does not prove the absence of action; it may show that an action was not recognized as recordable by the power of the time.

Fact assembly means the making of archives. Sources must be collected, selected, classified, catalogued, preserved, transferred, and sometimes digitized before they become accessible to researchers. An archive is not a naturally complete storehouse. It is an institutional mediation between social processes and historical narratives. Archival institutions determine what counts as an official document, what deserves long-term preservation, what may be destroyed, and how materials can be searched.

Fact retrieval means the making of narratives. Historians cannot consult everything that once existed. They work with materials that survived, were catalogued, were opened, and can be read within their linguistic and institutional capacities. Choices about archives, languages, keywords, chronology, and evidentiary standards also shape the narrative. A gap may therefore result from non-recording in the past, but it may also result from inaccessibility or from the historian’s own method of retrieval.

Retrospective significance is the production of history in its final instance. Holidays, museums, textbooks, state ceremonies, and public exhibitions decide again which events matter, what they should be called, and what political or national purposes they serve. The choice between “Columbus Day” and an Indigenous peoples’ commemoration is therefore not only a question about a date. It frames the meaning of colonization, conquest, nation, and memory.

The greatest challenge is not merely identifying a biased document. It is recognizing absences that have already been produced by archival institutions and inherited categories. The four moments are connected: an early absence reduces what enters the archive; archival organization shapes retrieval; retrieval shapes narrative; and public commemoration can make one narrative appear natural. I would therefore regard fact assembly and fact retrieval as especially difficult, because they determine what historians can actually see. Historians cannot completely overcome silence. They can make it visible, explain how it was produced, and use sources from different genres, languages, and positions to test one another. The goal is not to pretend that the past can be recovered in full, but to produce a more responsible narrative while acknowledging the limits of the archive.


问题二：第二章——“三个 Sans Souci”告诉我们什么？
Question 2: Chapter 2—What can the “Three Faces of Sans Souci” teach us about archives and sources?

中文答案

“三个 Sans Souci”是 Trouillot 说明历史可见性差异的典型案例。Jean-Baptiste Sans Souci 是海地革命中的黑人军事和政治参与者；Henry Christophe 是革命后成为重要统治者的领导人；Sans Souci 宫则是 Christophe 时期建造的宫殿和后来保存下来的纪念性遗址。三者共享一个名字，却在历史中留下了完全不同的痕迹。

Jean-Baptiste Sans Souci 的历史可见性较弱。他主要通过战争、口述传统、殖民或军事记录以及与其他领导人的冲突留下间接痕迹。这些材料往往不是他自己书写的，且通常由敌对或外部机构记录，因此他的声音和动机容易被压缩。相比之下，Henry Christophe 具有国家权力、正式职位和更稳定的政治机构，因而留下命令、外交文件、行政记录和后来的历史叙述。宫殿则作为实体建筑、旅游地点、图像和国家遗产继续存在。建筑比一个被杀害或被边缘化的革命者更容易被保存、拍摄、命名和展示。

这并不意味着宫殿比 Jean-Baptiste Sans Souci 更重要，而是说明历史可见性依赖于痕迹的类型和保存机制。一个名字可以从人的名字转化为建筑名称，再被后来的导览和历史书重新解释。纪念物保存了某种记忆，同时也可能遮蔽最初与这个名字相关的行动者。档案、纪念碑和以往历史并非简单地反映过去，它们会相互强化：已有的叙述指导档案选择，档案又使某些叙述看起来更可信。

当研究对象只在档案中微弱出现时，历史学家首先不应把“可见性”当作“重要性”的指标。应该把官方档案、地方材料、军事记录、私人文书、口述传统、建筑和地名放在一起阅读，追踪一个名字或事件在不同媒介中的变化。其次，应区分直接声音和他人转述，说明材料的生产者、目的和立场。最后，要对沉默保持克制：可以提出有根据的推论，但不能用研究者的想象替被沉默者发言。研究的任务既是恢复被忽略的行动者，也是解释他们为何难以被看见。

English answer

The “Three Faces of Sans Souci” is a case about unequal historical visibility. Jean-Baptiste Sans Souci was a Black military and political participant in the Haitian Revolution. Henry Christophe became a major leader and ruler after the revolution. The Sans Souci palace was a monumental building associated with Christophe and preserved as a physical site. They share a name, but they survive in history through very different traces.

Jean-Baptiste Sans Souci is comparatively difficult to see. He appears mainly through warfare, oral traditions, colonial or military reports, and accounts of conflict with other leaders. These records were generally produced by other people, sometimes by opponents or external institutions, so his own voice and intentions are easily compressed. Henry Christophe held formal authority and controlled more stable political institutions. He therefore left orders, diplomatic materials, administrative records, and a substantial later historiography. The palace survived as architecture, an image, a tourist site, and a national heritage object. A building is easier to preserve, photograph, name, and display than a revolutionary who was killed or marginalized.

This does not mean that the palace was historically more important than Jean-Baptiste Sans Souci. It shows that visibility depends on the kind of trace and on the institutions that preserve it. A personal name can be transferred to a building and then reinterpreted by guides, museums, and histories. A monument preserves one form of memory while obscuring the person originally connected with the name. Archives, monuments, and previous histories reinforce one another: established narratives influence what is collected, while preserved materials make those narratives appear more authoritative.

When a person appears only faintly in the archive, historians should not treat archival visibility as a measure of historical importance. They should read official records, local materials, military documents, private papers, oral traditions, buildings, and place names together, tracing how the name or event changes across media. They should distinguish a person’s direct voice from descriptions produced by others, and identify each source’s producer, purpose, and position. At the same time, historians must remain cautious. They may make evidence-based inferences, but they should not replace the absent voice with their own imagination. The task is both to recover overlooked actors and to explain why they became difficult to see.


问题三：第三章——Trouillot 所说的海地革命“不可思议”是什么意思？
Question 3: Chapter 3—What does Trouillot mean by calling the Haitian Revolution “unthinkable”?

中文答案

“不可思议”不是说海地革命没有发生，也不只是说欧洲人不知道这件事，而是说当时主流的知识和政治分类无法充分承认奴隶作为独立革命主体的能力。18 世纪末的圣多明各是法国殖民地，经济建立在大规模奴隶劳动上。法国大革命宣称自由和平等，但殖民社会又把非洲奴隶定义为财产、劳动力或需要被管理的对象。这样的概念框架很难容纳“被奴役者能够组织革命、制定政治目标并建立国家”这一事实。

欧洲观察者和殖民官员常常把起义解释为暴乱、野蛮行为、外部煽动或法国革命的次要后果，而不是奴隶自身的政治行动。这种解释不只是信息不足，而是预先决定了什么样的行动者被认为具有理性、文明和政治能力。海地革命因此在发生时就已经遭遇了认识论上的沉默。它后来也受到历史书写和国际政治的压低：承认一个由前奴隶建立的黑人共和国，会动摇欧洲殖民秩序和美洲奴隶制的合法性。

“不可思议”与“被遗忘”有区别。被遗忘意味着某件已能被理解的事情后来失去公共注意；不可思议则意味着事件发生时和被书写时，主流类别就难以让它成为一种合理、可想象的历史可能。遗忘可以通过重新发现档案部分修补；不可思议则要求历史学家反思自己使用的概念。

如果历史学家直接继承来源中的“种族”“文明”“革命”“国家”和“政治主体”等分类，就可能在无意中重复殖民者的假设。例如，只把法国白人政治家的文件视为理论，把海地革命者的行动视为情绪或暴力，就会把欧洲人的概念当成普遍标准。历史学家不能完全脱离自己的时代和语言，但可以采取反思性的做法：区分行动者的自我语言与行政标签；把法语官方材料与海地地方、军事、宗教、口述和物质材料互证；追问某一类别是谁创造的、服务谁，以及它排除了哪些可能的主体。使用来源不可避免地带入某些假设，但批判性阅读可以防止这些假设被伪装成自然事实。

English answer

By calling the Haitian Revolution “unthinkable,” Trouillot does not mean that it did not happen or that Europeans simply lacked information. He means that dominant political and intellectual categories could not adequately recognize enslaved people as independent revolutionary actors. In the late eighteenth century, Saint-Domingue was a French colony whose economy depended on large-scale slave labor. The French Revolution proclaimed liberty and equality, while colonial society defined Africans primarily as property, labor, or objects of administration. Within this framework it was difficult to accept that enslaved people could organize a revolution, formulate political aims, and create a state.

European observers and colonial officials often described the uprising as disorder, savagery, foreign manipulation, or a secondary consequence of the French Revolution rather than as political action by the enslaved themselves. This was not merely a lack of information. It was a set of assumptions about who could be rational, civilized, and politically capable. The revolution therefore encountered epistemological silence at the moment of its occurrence. Later historical writing and international politics also minimized it because recognizing a Black republic founded by formerly enslaved people threatened colonial order and the legitimacy of slavery in the Americas.

“Unthinkable” is different from “forgotten.” Forgetting occurs when an event that is already intelligible loses public attention. Unthinkability means that the dominant categories make the event difficult to recognize as a reasonable historical possibility in the first place. Rediscovering documents may partly repair forgetting, but confronting unthinkability also requires historians to revise the concepts through which they interpret evidence.

If historians simply inherit categories such as race, civilization, revolution, nation, and political agency from the societies and sources they study, they may reproduce colonial assumptions. For example, treating French political writers as producers of theory while treating Haitian revolutionaries as merely violent or emotional makes European categories into a universal standard. Historians cannot entirely escape their own languages and historical positions, but they can read sources reflexively. They can distinguish actors’ own vocabularies from administrative labels, compare French official records with Haitian local, military, religious, oral, and material evidence, and ask who created a category, whom it served, and whom it excluded. Sources inevitably carry assumptions, but critical reading can prevent those assumptions from appearing as natural facts.


问题四：第四章——记住某件事如何也能使过去沉默？
Question 4: Chapter 4—How can remembering something also silence the past?

中文答案

Trouillot 对哥伦布、1492 年、哥伦布日和 1992 年五百周年纪念的讨论说明，记忆不是沉默的对立面。一个事件被反复纪念，并不意味着所有相关历史都被看见。纪念往往通过选择人物、日期、名称、地理范围和道德语言，把复杂的过去压缩成一个便于传播的故事。

哥伦布是热那亚人，为西班牙王室航行；他的航行发生在加勒比，并且涉及原住民社会、欧洲帝国竞争、暴力、劳役和跨大西洋交流。然而，在美国主导的公共叙事中，他常常被纳入“发现美洲”的国家故事，仿佛加勒比、欧洲王室和原住民历史只是美国形成之前的背景。于是，“America”从一个多重地理和政治空间，被转化为美国民族故事中的预备阶段。

这种叙事使一些替代性的地理、行动者和历史变得不显眼：Taíno 等加勒比原住民不再是具有自身政治和社会历史的主体，而被写成被发现的对象；西班牙帝国和加勒比殖民地被压缩为美国故事的前史；非洲奴隶贸易、疾病、征服和劳役的历史被置于庆祝性叙事的边缘。1992 年五百周年纪念尤其暴露了“纪念”的政治性：它既可以庆祝航海和全球联系，也可以引发对殖民暴力和原住民生存的反思。

把“发现”改成“征服”是重要的，因为它改变了道德责任和行动者位置：被发现的土地变成了遭到占领的土地，哥伦布也从英雄变成征服过程中的参与者。但仅仅替换一个词，还不一定改变故事结构。如果故事仍然以欧洲航海者为起点，以欧洲行动决定历史方向，仍然把原住民和非洲人放在被动位置，那么它只是反转了道德评价，却没有真正去中心化。更充分的改写需要改变叙事的起点、地理尺度和主体：从加勒比社会自身出发，讨论原住民政治、欧洲殖民、非洲人口流动、环境变化以及不同群体的时间经验。

因此，准确日期、纪念名称和公共仪式都不是琐碎问题。它们决定谁被记住、谁被纪念、谁被视为历史的主角，也决定一个社会如何理解殖民主义和国家身份。

English answer

Trouillot’s discussion of Columbus, 1492, Columbus Day, and the 1992 quincentenary shows that memory is not simply the opposite of silence. An event may be repeatedly commemorated while many of its connected histories remain invisible. Commemoration compresses a complex past by selecting a person, a date, a name, a geography, and a moral vocabulary.

Columbus was a Genoese navigator sailing for the Spanish monarchy. His voyages took place in the Caribbean and involved Indigenous societies, European imperial competition, violence, forced labor, and Atlantic exchange. In a United States-centered public narrative, however, Columbus is often incorporated into a national story of the “discovery of America,” as if the Caribbean, the Spanish monarchy, and Indigenous histories were merely a prehistory of the United States. A multiple and contested geographic space is transformed into a preparatory stage in an American national story.

This narrative makes alternative geographies, actors, and histories less visible. Taíno and other Caribbean peoples cease to appear as political subjects with their own histories and become objects of discovery. The Spanish empire and the Caribbean colonies are reduced to a background for U.S. history. African slavery, disease, conquest, and forced labor are pushed to the edge of a celebratory story. The 1992 quincentenary made the politics of commemoration especially visible: it could celebrate navigation and global connection, but it could also provoke reflection on colonial violence and Indigenous survival.

Replacing “discovery” with “conquest” is significant because it changes moral responsibility and the position of the actors. The land is no longer an empty object waiting to be found, and Columbus becomes a participant in a process of occupation and domination. Yet changing one word does not necessarily change the structure of the story. If Europe’s navigator remains the starting point, if European action still determines the direction of history, and if Indigenous and African peoples remain passive objects, then the change merely reverses the moral judgment. A deeper revision must change the narrative’s starting point, geographic scale, and subjects by beginning with Caribbean societies and examining Indigenous politics, European colonization, African movement, environmental change, and different experiences of time.

The date, the name of a holiday, and the form of a public ceremony therefore matter. They distribute historical visibility. They decide who is remembered as an actor, who is honored, and how a society understands colonialism and national identity.


问题五：全书——读完 Trouillot 后，历史学家应当做出哪些改变？
Question 5: Across the book—What should historians do differently after reading Trouillot?

中文答案

把 Sans Souci、海地革命和哥伦布/哥伦布日放在一起，可以看到它们共同说明：权力不是只在历史叙事写成之后才出现，而是在来源、档案、叙事和历史意义的整个链条中发挥作用。

Sans Souci 的案例显示，人的历史可见性取决于什么样的痕迹被保存。Jean-Baptiste Sans Souci 作为革命参与者留下的材料较为间接，而 Henry Christophe 和 Sans Souci 宫因为政治权力、行政机构和物质建筑而更容易进入后来的历史。这个案例提醒我们，档案中出现得多的人不一定比出现得少的人更重要。历史学家必须追踪地名、建筑、口述、军事记录和敌对者的叙述，寻找被国家档案遮蔽的行动者。

海地革命则揭示更深层的“不可思议”。问题并不只是材料缺少，而是殖民社会的种族和文明分类让欧洲观察者难以承认奴隶具有革命能力和政治主体性。即使档案很多，如果历史学家把殖民者的分类当作自然事实，也会重复这种沉默。

哥伦布案例进一步说明，公共纪念会把选择性叙事固定为常识。把加勒比的复杂历史纳入美国“发现美洲”的故事，能够突出美国民族身份，却压低原住民、非洲人、西班牙殖民地和加勒比社会的历史。即使把“发现”改为“征服”，如果叙事仍然以欧洲航海者为中心，也只是改变道德评价，没有改变权力结构。

这三个案例共同告诉我们，权力、来源、档案、叙事和历史意义是相互构成的关系。来源不是事件的自然镜像；档案不是中立仓库；叙事不是材料的机械排列；意义也不是事件本身自动携带的属性。历史学家在选择材料、安排章节、确定关键词、决定研究对象和解释重要性时，确实参与了历史意义的生产。

因此，历史学家至少承担四项责任。第一，说明证据是如何产生和保存的，而不是只把文件当作透明事实。第二，主动寻找被压低的声音，并承认不同群体拥有不平等的记录和访问条件。第三，对自己的概念和叙事位置保持反思，避免把殖民、国家或精英机构的分类伪装成普遍标准。第四，区分证据支持的推论和无法证实的想象，不因为想填补沉默就替缺席者发言。

但 Trouillot 也规定了历史学家的限度。历史学家不能创造不存在的来源，不能保证恢复全部过去，也不能通过“加入更多声音”就消除所有不平等。档案缺口有时只能被解释为缺口，而不能被填满。研究者能做的是把沉默的形成过程写进研究，交代自己的选择和限制，并通过多种来源、不同尺度和不同叙事方式提出更诚实、更可讨论的历史。

结合我自己的报纸和政府文件阅读经验，这意味着我不能把一页报纸当成单一的“社会意见”。我需要区分官方公告、编辑选择、署名评论、匿名意见和数据库的数字化安排，并询问谁有能力发表、谁被排除，以及某种医学或行政语言怎样把争议转化为可管理的问题。这样，历史研究的目标就不只是发现新的事实，也包括在既有材料中建立新的、负责任的叙事关系。

English answer

Placing Sans Souci, the Haitian Revolution, and Columbus and Columbus Day together shows that power does not appear only after a historical narrative has been written. It operates throughout the chain connecting sources, archives, narratives, and historical significance.

The Sans Souci case shows that historical visibility depends on the kinds of traces that survive. Jean-Baptiste Sans Souci appears mainly through indirect accounts, while Henry Christophe and the Sans Souci palace are more visible because political office, administrative institutions, and architecture generated and preserved more durable traces. A person who appears frequently in the archive is not necessarily more important than one who appears rarely. Historians should therefore follow place names, buildings, oral traditions, military records, and hostile accounts in order to identify actors obscured by state-centered archives.

The Haitian Revolution reveals a deeper form of silence: unthinkability. The problem was not simply a lack of evidence. Colonial racial and civilizational categories made it difficult for European observers to recognize enslaved people as capable of revolutionary action and political agency. Even a large archive can reproduce this silence if historians treat colonial classifications as natural facts.

The Columbus case shows how public commemoration can turn a selective narrative into common sense. Incorporating the Caribbean into a U.S. story of the “discovery of America” strengthens a national identity while reducing the visibility of Indigenous peoples, Africans, Spanish colonial societies, and Caribbean histories. Replacing “discovery” with “conquest” changes the moral evaluation, but it may leave the structure intact if the European navigator remains the center of the story. A genuine revision must change the geography, chronology, and subjects of the narrative.

Together, these cases show that power, sources, archives, narratives, and significance are mutually constitutive. Sources are not natural mirrors of events; archives are not neutral storehouses; narratives are not mechanical arrangements of evidence; and significance is not automatically contained in an event. Historians participate in producing historical meaning when they choose materials, organize chapters, define categories, select subjects, and decide what matters.

After reading Trouillot, historians therefore have at least four responsibilities. First, they should explain how evidence was produced, selected, and preserved instead of treating documents as transparent facts. Second, they should look actively for marginalized voices while acknowledging unequal conditions of recording and access. Third, they should examine their own concepts and narrative position, rather than presenting colonial, national, or elite categories as universal standards. Fourth, they should distinguish evidence-based inference from speculation and should not speak for absent people simply in order to fill a gap.

Trouillot also establishes limits. Historians cannot create sources that never existed, guarantee a complete recovery of the past, or eliminate every inequality by adding more voices. Some archival gaps can only be represented as gaps. Historians can, however, write the production of silence into their research, disclose their choices and limitations, and construct more honest and debatable histories through multiple sources, scales, and narrative forms.

In my own reading of newspapers and government documents, this means that I cannot treat a newspaper page as a single expression of public opinion. I need to distinguish an official notice, editorial selection, signed commentary, anonymous contribution, and the digital database through which I now read the page. I should ask who could publish, who was excluded, and how medical or administrative language transformed disagreement into a manageable problem. Historical research should therefore not only seek new facts; it should also create new and responsible relationships among existing evidence, silence, and narrative.


简短课堂总结
Short classroom summary

中文：Trouillot 的主要教训是，历史不是过去的透明镜像，而是权力在来源制作、档案保存、叙事组织和公共纪念中持续生产的结果。历史学家无法完全消除沉默，但可以识别沉默如何形成、谁因此失去可见性，并在不替缺席者虚构发言的前提下，建立更开放、更自觉的历史叙事。

English: Trouillot’s central lesson is that history is not a transparent mirror of the past. It is produced through power operating in the creation of sources, the assembly of archives, the construction of narratives, and the assignment of public significance. Historians cannot eliminate silence completely, but they can identify how it was produced, who became less visible, and how to construct a more open and self-critical narrative without inventing a voice for the absent.
