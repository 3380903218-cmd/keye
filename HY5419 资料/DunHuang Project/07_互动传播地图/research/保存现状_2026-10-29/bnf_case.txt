L302@P4: Conservators often need to revisit conservation undertaken
L303@P4: by their predecessors and consider new treatments. This might
L304@P4: be for reasons of preservation, of text legibility or aesthetic
L305@P4: reasons, but also, as in the two examples discussed here, when
L306@P4: twentieth-century conservation has compromised the document
L307@P4: and concealed its archaeological value.
L308@P4: The two manuscripts under discussion were chosen for their
L309@P4: two different codex forms, butterfly and whirlwind. Both are
L310@P4: manuals with texts that would have been regularly consulted:
L311@P4: Pelliot Chinois 2547 (P.2547, pictured opposite) is a booklet of
L312@P4: texts to be recited on different occasions; Pelliot Chinois 2490
L313@P4: (P.2490, pictured above) is a table for calculating farming land.
L314@P4: The damaged and fragmentary leaves of both manuscripts, the
L315@P4: numerous contemporary repairs on P.2547, and the changes in
L316@P4: binding are all evidence of their repeated use. Booklets that were
L317@P4: easier to handle than scrolls seem to have been preferred for this
L318@P4: kind of text.1
L319@P4: Description of the manuscripts
L320@P4: P.2547: Entitled Zhaiwan wen 齋琬文 (Texts of purification
L321@P4: and lament), this is a booklet of ‘texts and formulae used for
L322@P4: various rituals’ according to its anonymous author. It is likely
L323@P4: that the texts were composed between 647 and 788, and it is
L324@P4: estimated that this copy was written between 728 and 744.2
L325@P4: The manuscript was originally a scroll that was cut into fifteen
L326@P4: bifolios and mounted on guards and so made into a booklet with
L327@P4: butterfly binding.3 It is 280mm high, but the original length of
L328@P4: the bifolios is unknown as they are severely damaged on the left,
L329@P4: most-handled side opposite the roller.
L330@P4: The manuscript is on very even, buff mulberry paper. Before
L331@P4: assembly, each of the folded leaves was mounted on a paper
L332@P4: strip folded in two, called a guard, and pasted to each bifolio at
L333@P4: the blank edge so that about a centimetre of the guard was left
L334@P4: protruding. The mounting was necessary to make the text visible
L335@P4: when the leaves were assembled and stitched.The guards, made
L336@P4: of a yellow-tinted soft mulberry paper4 similar to those dyed
L337@P4: with huangbo 黄檗 (Phellodendron amurense),were cut from a
L338@P4: manuscript made from recycled paper that was a land register
L339@P4: from 747 according to the date visible on one of the guards.We
L340@P4: can therefore conjecture that the mounting was added between
L341@P4: 762 and the occupation of this region by the Tibetans (786-848),
L342@P4: during which time such texts were probably not used. 5 It is certainly an early, and possibly the first, example of butterfly binding.
L343@P4: The folded sheets were stacked, forming a block by pasting
L344@P4: together all the guards. This block of guards was placed between
L345@P4: the two halves of a small branch of tamarisk,6 10 mm in diameter
L346@P4: and 326mm long, covered in a red-brown glossy substance that
L347@P4: is now very cracked, and is not lacquer, despite its appearance.7
L348@P4: The guards were stitched to it using hemp thread. An old repair
L349@P4: could be seen.8
L350@P4: The document shows evidence of contemporary conservation
L351@P4: using paper recycled from old manuscripts with the original text
L352@P4: side pasted to the recto of the folio. They are made of homogeneous mulberry paper, thicker than that of the document.
L353@P4: and in 1954 ten pieces were removed to enable their text to
L354@P4: be read. They were preserved separately. Also, all the folios were
L355@P4: strengthened on their blank sides, and gaps were filled with fine
L356@P4: paper, assuming a uniform folio size, that of the longest preserved.
L357@P4: Then a second lining of silk gauze was placed on both faces of
L358@P4: the bifolios, concealing the text and significantly increasing the
L359@P4: thickness of the document from the roller.

L413@P5: This survey has shown that previous
L414@P5: curators and conservators attempted to
L415@P5: preserve rare texts by consolidating and
L416@P5: fixing all fragments that could potentially
L417@P5: be lost. They did not however consider
L418@P5: the impact of their interventions on the
L419@P5: original document. We now consider these
L420@P5: interventions as excessive due to the stress
L421@P5: they exerted on the original document,
L422@P5: and thus need to consider new treatments.
L423@P5: Conservation Treatments
L424@P5: Initial treatment consisted of removing
L425@P5: the linings added in 1954 and 1964 from
L426@P5: the two manuscripts to allow the folios
L427@P5: to be flattened and recover flexibility,
L428@P5: making the text more legible. We hoped
L429@P5: to return the manuscripts to the state in
L430@P5: which they originally entered the collections of the BnF.
L431@P5: In particular, we decided to preserve
L432@P5: the original binding of P.2547, but not
L433@P5: the whirlwind binding of P.2490, the latter
L434@P5: being a twentieth-century reconstructed
L435@P5: binding for which we have no record,
L436@P5: and as such holds no scholarly interest for
L437@P5: the historian or conservator. Neither did
L438@P5: we try to restore the folio length of these
L439@P5: two documents, which cannot be known
L440@P5: with accuracy. We strengthened the paper
L441@P5: where it was weak, split or damaged, as
L442@P5: handling was impossible or dangerous for
L443@P5: the integrity of the texts. Pieces of paper
L444@P5: containing text that had been added to
L445@P5: P.2547 during contemporary conservation
L446@P5: and had been removed in the 1950s and
L447@P5: preserved separately were not returned to
L448@P5: the manuscript, as we could not be sure
L449@P5: of their original position. We did however
L450@P5: leave contemporary conservation paper
L451@P5: discovered in the process of reversing
L452@P5: twentieth-century treatments.
L453@P5: The reversing of modern treatments has
L454@P5: allowed us to rediscover seals and original
L455@P5: conservation treatments that were left in
L456@P5: situ under the modern linings as they were
L457@P5: thought to contain no text, and so not
L458@P5: worth removing at the time. However, one
L459@P5: of the paper fragments bears the characters
L460@P5: of the name of the Lingtu monastery 靈
L461@P5: 圖寺 in Dunhuang. This small piece of
L462@P5: paper, together with three others, also
L463@P5: bears a fragment of an unidentifiable seal
L464@P5: and was attached to the manuscript with
L465@P5: only a few drops of paste. These pieces
L466@P5: came unstuck during our treatment and
L467@P5: will be remounted on guards at their exact
L468@P5: original position so that the fragments of
L469@P5: text and seal will still be visible. Also, a
L470@P5: fragment classified as Pelliot Chinois 4072
L471@P5: (2) was found to be a missing part of the
L472@P5: last folio of P.2547, and will be rejoined
L473@P5: to this manuscript.
