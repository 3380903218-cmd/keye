Abstract
摘要
The case study concerns Singapore's Chinatown which occupies an important place in the country's
history and has acquired major significance as a tourist attraction. Whilst much of the area was saved
from the urban redevelopment characteristic of Singapore in the period since its independence and its
conservation has become an official priority, approaches adopted towards the presentation and
interpretation of the site have changed over time and are now a matter of public debate. Interest has
increased recently as a result of the announcement of a new development programme which is outlined
after a brief history of Chinatown. An account of the responses and concerns about the future then
follows. Despite the very distinctive characteristics of Chinatown, events there raise general questions
about the use of heritage as a tourist attraction including matters of authenticity and local involvement
as well as those relating to the applicability of the life cycle model; these themes are addressed in the
final section which presents some general conclusions based on the particular circumstances of the
case. ( 2000 Elsevier Science Ltd. All rights reserved.
本案例研究讨论新加坡牛车水。牛车水在新加坡历史中占有重要位置，同时也已成为重要旅游景点。新
加坡独立以来的大规模城市重建并未完全摧毁这一地区，而牛车水保护后来也成为官方政策重点；不过
，对该地区进行展示与阐释的方式不断变化，并逐渐成为公共讨论议题。随着一项新的发展计划公布，
人们对牛车水的关注进一步上升。文章先简要回顾牛车水的历史，再介绍该计划，并讨论社会各方对未
来的回应和担忧。尽管牛车水具有非常独特的特征，但这里发生的事情提出了一些更普遍的问题：文化
遗产被用作旅游景点时，如何处理真实性、地方参与，以及旅游目的地生命周期模型是否适用等问题。
文章最后结合牛车水的具体情况讨论这些主题，并提出较为一般性的结论。

1. Introduction
1. 引言
The Chinese diaspora has resulted in a large population living outside of China, principally in Asia with
significant numbers in Europe and North America. It has been estimated that this population numbered
over 30 million in 1990 (Pan, 1998) and continued expansion is likely to have taken place since then.
Early Chinese settlers overseas often gathered together in communities, many of which still exist as
Chinatowns throughout the world and represent important tourist sites in cities including London, San
Francisco, Sydney, Vancouver and Yokohama. The characteristics of such ethnic enclaves as living
communities and tourist attractions has recently attracted the interest of academics like Conforti (1996)
whose studies address questions of place presentation, promotion, planning and participation. Many of
these issues are raised by the case of Singapore's Chinatown which has played an important part in the
country's history, with an official commitment to its conservation since the 1980s. It continues to evolve
as a living and working community, but that community is under threat from tourism as the government
intensifies efforts to develop and promote it as a destination for tourists with the launch of an ambitious
programme for its revitalisation which critics have argued will turn the area into a theme park. This
study considers some of the important features of Chinatown's history and changes in attitudes towards
its conservation. The final section returns to the broader themes of participatory planning, authenticity
and destination life cycles in an attempt to extend the discussion and permit some more general
conclusions to be derived. While acknowledging the uniqueness of Singapore's Chinatown, these would
appear to be matters that affect many such areas of this type and have relevance for the continuing
debate about urban heritage conservation in general and the use of that which is conserved as a tourist
attraction.
华人离散使大量华人生活在中国之外，主要分布于亚洲，同时欧洲和北美也有相当数量的华人。据估计
，1990年海外华人人口已超过三千万（Pan, 1998），此后很可能继续增长。早期海外华人往往聚居形
成社区，其中许多至今仍以“Chinatown（唐人街/华人街区）”的形式存在于世界各地，并成为伦敦、
旧金山、悉尼、温哥华和横滨等城市的重要旅游地点。此类族裔聚居区既是生活社区，又是旅游景点，
其特征近年来引起了 Conforti（1996）等学者关注，相关研究涉及地方展示、推广、规划和参与等问
题。新加坡牛车水正集中体现了这些议题。牛车水在新加坡历史上发挥过重要作用，自20世纪80年代以
来，政府正式承诺对其进行保护。它仍在作为一个有人生活和工作的社区不断演变，但随着政府加强把
它开发和推广为旅游目的地，这一社区也受到旅游业的压力；批评者认为，雄心勃勃的“复兴”计划可
能把这里变成主题公园。本文考察牛车水历史的一些重要特征以及人们对保护问题态度的变化。最后一
部分回到参与式规划、真实性和目的地生命周期等更广泛的议题，尝试扩展讨论并提出一些更具普遍性
的结论。尽管新加坡牛车水具有独特性，这些问题显然也影响许多同类地区，并与城市遗产保护以及把
受保护遗产作为旅游景点使用的持续争论相关。
2. A brief history
2. 简史
2.1. The origins of Chinatown
2.1 牛车水的起源
Chinatown was created in the early decades of the 19th century when Stamford Raffles, who had
claimed Singapore for the British in 1819, set up a Town Committee to plan a new town which would
accommodate the many different ethnic groups who had been attracted to the port and set aside areas
for commercial and civic use. He was concerned about the rapid expansion of the original settlement
and wanted to impose some order on its development. The Chinese were allocated a town south of the
Singapore River with a kampong adjoining to allow for further growth, beyond that there was
unexplored forest and jungle. Other races including the Chuliahs, Arabs and Bugis were located in
kampongs, or villages, north of the river with the central district given over to the European Town,

administration and business. Chinatown grew quickly as immigrants arrived mainly from the southern
Chinese provinces with a variety of dialect groups such as the Hokkiens, Teochews, Cantonese,
Hainanese, Hakkas and Foochows which each formed their own communities. Such a mix is reflected in
the cuisine of today, a series of different temples and a variety of clan associations. The distinctive
architecture of shophouses which they created where the ground floor is used as shop space while the
owners live above was influenced by Chinese Fujian style as well as that of the Straits Chinese who
came from Malacca. Plots of land were divided into long narrow strips and houses built stretching far
back for as much as 45.7 m and fronted by a pedestrian walkway or five foot way. Shophouses display
great diversity from the basic original Early Shophouse Style (1840-1900) to the more flamboyant Late
Shophouse Style (1900-1940) and those that remain are a feature of great interest to tourists (Urban
Redevelopment Authority, undated). From a population of just over 1000 in 1821, numbers reached
almost 14,000 in 1836, 25 and 46 per cent of Singapore's total, respectively, with contemporary maps
indicating a period of intense urbanisation stimulated by the trading opportunities brought to the port
as a result of the opening of the Suez Canal. By the turn of the century, Chinatown had spread south
and west with some land reclamation offshore and the population was 164,041. It was in these years
that many important cultural institutions were founded, several of which are still notable landmarks.
Also at this time, the noise and congestion became too much for many residents and the more affluent
started to move out.
牛车水形成于19世纪初期。1819年斯坦福·莱佛士代表英国取得新加坡后，成立城市委员会规划新城，
以容纳被港口吸引而来的不同族群，并划定商业与市政用途的空间。他担忧最初聚落迅速扩张，希望为
城市发展建立秩序。华人被安排在新加坡河以南的城镇区域，并在旁边划出甘榜以供未来扩展；再往外
则仍是未经开发的森林。Chulia、阿拉伯人和武吉士等其他族群被安排在河以北的甘榜，而中心地区则
作为欧洲人城镇、行政与商业区。随着移民主要从中国南方各省抵达，牛车水迅速成长；福建、潮州、
广东、海南、客家和福州等不同方言群体分别形成各自社群。这种多样性今天仍可从饮食、不同庙宇以
及各种宗乡会馆中看到。移民建造的店屋具有鲜明特色：底层用于经商，上层供店主居住，其建筑既受
福建风格影响，也受到来自马六甲的海峡华人影响。地块被划成长而窄的形状，房屋可向后延伸45.7米
，前方设有供行人通行的“五脚基”。店屋风格十分多样，从较朴素的早期店屋风格（1840-1900）到
更加华丽的晚期店屋风格（1900-1940），保存下来的店屋今天成为很受游客关注的景观（Urban
Redevelopment Authority, undated）。1821年牛车水人口刚过一千，至1836年已接近一万四千，分别
约占当时新加坡总人口的25%和46%。同期地图显示，随着苏伊士运河开通给港口贸易带来机会，这里经
历了强烈城市化。到20世纪之交，牛车水已向南和向西扩展，部分沿海土地经过填海，人口达到164,04
1人。许多重要文化机构也在这一时期成立，其中一些今天仍是著名地标。与此同时，噪音和拥挤使不
少居民难以忍受，较富裕者开始迁出。

2.2. Living conditions
2.2 生活条件
As the population expanded, there were mounting concerns about living conditions in Chinatown with
evidence of overcrowding, congestion and pollution. Many inhabitants were coolies or labourers who
shared the same sleeping space in cubicles in coolie lodging houses with little sanitation and limited
access to fresh water. They faced poverty, malnutrition and had no proper medical facilities, despite the
high incidence of contagious diseases including tuberculosis. In addition, secret societies, gang crime,
opium smoking and prostitution were rampant. The area became the centre of the Chinese coolie trade
where unsuspecting coolies were bought and sold, then shipped on overseas. The streets were also full
of hawkers selling a variety of goods from cooked food to household wares, operating on foot all day
and later crowding narrow passages with their stalls. Yet early travellers were drawn to Chinatown and
found street life and the roadside culture colourful and entertaining, while still being shocked by the
squalor present in many parts. It seemed to epitomise the exotic Orient and ‘the diversity of forms,
shapes, colours and people of the street scenes best reinforced the Westerner's romantic conception of
the picturesque' (Savage, 1992, p. 17). Conditions deteriorated further after the Second World War and
Japanese Occupation when parts of the town were badly bombed, destroying homes and businesses.

There was a government enquiry in 1947 (Kiat, 1947) and a later report by Kaye (1960) documented
the appalling life for many inhabitants of Chinatown. The particular concern was about overcrowding
which was partly a result of the subtenanting practice of dividing and further subdividing rooms, the
most dilapidated buildings often the worst affected. The back-to-back rows upon rows of shophouses
had steep staircases leading to the living quarters above which were divided by partitions into cubicles,
about the width of two double beds. Cubicles were often shared by households with as many as seven
family members. There was a common kitchen, bathroom and lavatory in each building and originally
night soil had to be carried through the house when collected because there were no back lanes. It is
perhaps worth bearing these facts in mind in any discussion of the restoration of Chinatown and the
invitation of promotional literature to come and see Chinatown as it once was.
随着人口增长，人们越来越担忧牛车水的生活环境，尤其是过度拥挤、交通堵塞和污染。许多居民是苦
力或劳工，住在苦力宿舍狭小的隔间里，共用睡眠空间，卫生设施不足，获得清洁用水也很有限。他们
面对贫困和营养不良，并缺乏适当医疗设施，而结核病等传染病又十分常见。此外，秘密会社、帮派犯
罪、吸食鸦片和卖淫等现象也很普遍。牛车水成为华工贸易中心，许多毫无戒心的苦力被买卖后再运往
海外。街道上也挤满小贩，他们从熟食到家居用品什么都卖，白天挑担流动经营，稍晚又在狭窄通道摆
摊。尽管如此，早期旅行者仍被牛车水吸引，认为街头生活和路边文化色彩丰富、很有趣，同时又震惊
于不少地方的肮脏贫困。这里似乎体现了西方想象中的“异域东方”，正如 Savage（1992, p.17）所
说，街景中形式、形状、色彩与人的多样性强化了西方人对“如画景致”的浪漫想象。第二次世界大战
和日占时期之后，情况进一步恶化，部分街区遭严重轰炸，住宅和商业设施被毁。政府于1947年展开调
查（Kiat, 1947），Kaye（1960）后来的一份报告也记录了许多牛车水居民恶劣的生活状况。其中最受
关注的是过度拥挤，这部分源于转租制度：房间被一再分割，越破旧的建筑往往越拥挤。一排排背靠背
的店屋有陡峭楼梯通往上层居住区，上层又被隔板分成狭小房间，每间大约只有两张双人床并排的宽度
，却可能由多达七人的家庭共同使用。每栋楼通常只有共用厨房、浴室和厕所；最初因为没有后巷，清
粪时甚至要把粪桶从屋内搬出去。今天在讨论牛车水修复，以及旅游宣传邀请游客“来看从前的牛车水
”时，这些事实值得牢记。
2.3. Change and improvement
2.3 变化与改善
Some improvement was attempted by the colonial authorities, but significant change only came with
Singapore's independence firstly from the British and then from the Malayan Federation in 1965. The
government was committed to raising the living standards of the population and the public provision of
better housing became a key objective. A number of development agencies and United Nations advisers
were also proposing comprehensive urban renewal programmes which demanded extensive land
clearance, especially in the city centre. The Urban Renewal Department of the Housing and
Development Board, the forerunner of the present Urban Redevelopment Authority (URA), was given
responsibility for Chinatown and started a programme of redevelopment and resettlement in
association with other official agencies. Hawkers were gradually cleared off the streets and relocated in
permanent hawker centres while shophouses were demolished to make way for blocks of flats, a policy
that continued from the 1960s until the 1980s and created great changes to the appearance,
atmosphere and activities of Chinatown. Part of the philosophy underlying this policy was the
integration of ethnic communities to forge a sense of national identity as different racial groups were
placed together in the new public housing blocks, representing a complete break with the past and the
Raffles plan of ethnic enclaves. Chinatown thus occupies a special place in the history of Singapore. It
owes its origin to Stamford Raffles, generally considered the founder of modern Singapore, and grew
with the port to become a centre of the trade and commercial activity responsible for Singapore's early
prosperity. It indicates the contribution that the Chinese have made to the country and the strength of
their culture. From the beginning, it also functioned as a tourist attraction with a fascination for many
westerners. However, it came to symbolise a past that the new republic of Singapore wished to move
on from, with the area having no role in the modern city state that the government planned and was in
a hurry to realise. There was little sympathy with the notion of conservation of what was considered a
crowded and unhealthy district where the trades conducted did not belong to a 20th century ‘tropical

city of excellence' (Liu, 1997, p. 31), the phrase adopted by the planning authorities to describe their
future vision of Singapore. There was no legislation or overall government policy on conservation, few
incentives to conserve and a reluctance amongst investors to put money into conservation. The official
view was expressed by the Minister for National Development who said in 1980 that it was not practical
to preserve most of the buildings in Chinatown because of their poor structural condition (Powell, 1994).
Conservation was considered incompatible with development and prevented an area from reaching its
full economic potential.
殖民当局曾尝试改善环境，但真正显著的变化要到新加坡先脱离英国、继而于1965年脱离马来西亚联邦
实现独立后才出现。政府致力于提高民众生活水平，公共住房供应成为关键目标。多个发展机构以及联
合国顾问也提出综合城市更新计划，要求大规模清理土地，尤其是市中心地区。建屋发展局下属的城市
更新部门——即今天市区重建局（URA）的前身——负责牛车水，并与其他官方机构合作开展重建和居
民安置。街头小贩逐渐被迁往固定的熟食中心，店屋被拆除以兴建组屋；这项政策从20世纪60年代持续
到80年代，极大改变了牛车水的外观、氛围和日常活动。其政策理念之一，是把不同族群安排到同一批
新建公共住房中，以推动族群融合并塑造国家认同，从而彻底打破过去莱佛士规划下的族群聚居模式。
牛车水因此在新加坡历史中占据特殊地位。它源于通常被视为现代新加坡奠基者的斯坦福·莱佛士，并
随着港口发展成为贸易和商业中心，为新加坡早期繁荣作出贡献；它也体现了华人对国家的贡献及其文
化力量。从一开始，牛车水还因其对许多西方人的吸引力而具有旅游功能。然而，对新成立的新加坡共
和国而言，它又象征着一个国家急于摆脱的过去；在政府计划快速实现的现代城市国家蓝图中，这一地
区似乎没有位置。对于保护一个被视为拥挤、不卫生、其传统行业不属于20世纪“卓越热带城市”（Li
u, 1997, p.31）的地区，当时几乎没有同情。彼时既缺乏保护立法和总体政府政策，也缺乏保护激励
措施，投资者同样不愿为保护投入资金。1980年国家发展部长就表达了官方立场：由于牛车水大多数建
筑结构状况太差，保留并不实际（Powell,
1994）。保护被视为与发展相冲突，会阻碍地区充分发挥经济潜力。

3. Conservation in the 1980s
3. 20世纪80年代的遗产保护
3.1. A new attitude
3.1 新态度
There was a change in attitude in the 1980s as officials recognised the value of conservation and
popular awareness of the possible disappearance of irreplaceable aspects of the country's heritage
increased, an indication of the latter being the founding of the Singapore Heritage Society in 1998.
Chinatown was acknowledged as a unique district, worthy of preservation in the interests of the
resident population, but also as a tourist site. The year of 1984 was seen as a turning point when the
government accepted a report by the URA setting up a pilot conservation project in the Tanjong Pagar
district, one especially rich in shophouse architecture. Smith (1988) has explained the shift in
government policy towards conservation as a result of a sudden decline in visitor arrivals and its impact
on hotel occupancy levels. Inbound tourism had been increasing at an average rate in excess of 10 per
cent up until 1982 when there was a fall of 4.5 per cent, leading to the danger of overcapacity in the
accommodation sector as hotels were doubling their room stock in anticipation of continued growth. In
response to this potential crisis, the government appointed a Tourism Task Force to consider the
problems and possible solutions. The Report of the Tourism Task Force in 1984 (Wong et al., 1984)
highlighted the three main problems of perceptions of Singapore as an expensive destination with few
tourist attractions and adversely affected by foreign protectionism. It recognised that some historical
sites had disappeared because of development and there was a subsequent risk of Singapore losing its
distinctive heritage which appealed to many visitors. In the words of the report, ‘in our effort to build up
a modern metropolis, we have removed aspects of our Oriental mystique and charm which are best
symbolised in old buildings, traditional activities and bustling road side activities' (p.6). There was thus
a strong economic case for preserving and restoring Chinatown and other historical sites, one of the

20世纪80年代，官方态度发生变化：政府开始认识到保护的价值，社会也越来越意识到国家某些不可替
代的遗产可能消失；这种社会意识后来体现在新加坡遗产学会的成立等方面。牛车水被承认为一个独特
地区，不仅应为居民利益而保存，也具有旅游价值。1984年通常被视为转折点，当年政府接受市区重建
局的一份报告，在店屋建筑尤其丰富的丹戎巴葛启动保护试点项目。Smith（1988）认为，政府政策转
向遗产保护与游客数量突然下降以及由此造成的酒店入住率压力有关。入境旅游此前一直以年均超过10
%的速度增长，但1982年游客人数下降4.5%；当时酒店正因预期旅游持续增长而大幅扩充客房，因此出
现供给过剩风险。政府为此成立旅游工作小组研究问题和可能的解决办法。1984年的《旅游工作小组报
告》（Wong et al., 1984）指出三项主要问题：新加坡被认为价格昂贵、旅游吸引物不足，以及受到
海外保护主义的不利影响。报告承认，一些历史地点已因发展而消失，新加坡因此可能失去吸引游客的
独特遗产。报告写道：“在建设现代大都市的努力中，我们去除了东方的神秘感和魅力，而这些最能由
老建筑、传统活动和热闹的路边生活来体现”（p.6）。因此，保存和修复牛车水及其他历史地点具有
强烈经济理由，这也成为报告的主要建议之一。
3.2. Conservation initiatives
3.2 保护举措
Conservation became a key theme of subsequent studies such as those commissioned by the Singapore
Tourist Promotion Board (STPB) from Pannell, Kerr, Forster Consultants (1986) and another prepared by
the Pacific Area Travel Association (PATA, 1985) which together with the Task Force report formed the
basis for the official Tourism Product Development Plan prepared in 1986 (MTI and STPB, 1986). The
Plan was a joint initiative of the STPB and the Ministry of Trade and Industry and proposed using six
themes to promote and develop Singapore as a tourist destination with the intention of attracting more
visitors and spending. The Exotic East and Colonial Heritage were themes which involved the
conservation of various historic areas and places including Chinatown and incorporated proposals for its
revitalisation. A Conservation Plan was devised for Chinatown which had the following objectives: (a) To
retain and restore buildings of historical and architectural significance. (b) To improve the general
physical environment and to introduce appropriate new features to further enhance the identity of the
area. (c) To retain and enhance ethnic-based activities while consolidating the area with new and
compatible activities. (d) To involve both the public and private sectors in carrying out conservation
projects. Conservation has since been carried out by a combination of measures. The URA completed
the Tanjong Pagar pilot scheme and had restored 32 shophouses by 1988 while government purchased
derelict and poorly maintained buildings and sites which were put out to tender for private conservation
under the supervision of the URA. Existing owners were also encouraged to conserve their properties,
again supervised by the URA. The implementation of the plan is described by the URA its guide to the
Historic District of Chinatown (1995) where it claims ‘with many of the fundamentals taken care of, its
attention is now focused on quality restoration' (p. 1). A measure of its success was the gazetting, or
official listing, of Chinatown as a conservation area in 1989 which guarantees a degree of protection
from development and conformity to certain standards and procedures with regard to alterations in use
and appearance. As the plan progressed, changes became obvious with the restoration of rows of
shophouses and the arrival of new businesses and private tenants. Areas were also pedestrianised and
environmental improvements made such as tree planting and other forms of landscaping. At the same
time, Chinatown was given greater prominence in the Tourist Promotion Board's marketing and
advertising campaigns. Tour companies were encouraged to include it in their itineraries and formally
registered guides expected to be knowledgeable about the area's history, buildings and traditions.
These developments were not without their critics who complained that the Chinatown being conserved
and promoted lacked the character and authenticity of the original. High rents for business and
residential properties were making it impossible for local people to live and work there, and genuine
traditional trades were disappearing to be replaced by fashionable restaurants, expensive boutiques,
souvenir shops and smart offices. Gentrification appeared to be taking place with local people gaining
little benefit and actually at risk of disinheritance. By the end of the 1980s then, Chinatown was
assuming a new importance as a heritage site of significance for both residents and visitors and this
was part of a wider process of increased awareness of and commitment to heritage conservation

URA (1991a) were merged and a single Concept Plan produced for the island (1991b). The subject was

especially within the context of Chinatown. Such concerns intensified in the 1990s with Chinatown's
continued promotion as a tourist attraction and more criticisms of it being turned into a theme park
from which locals were excluded.

剥夺自身文化与空间的风险。到20世纪80年代末，牛车水已作为对居民和游客都重要的遗产地点获得新

尤其集中在牛车水。90年代随着牛车水继续被作为旅游景点推广，关于它正在被改造成主题公园、本地
居民被排除在外的批评反而更强。

4. Chinatown in the 1990s
4. 20世纪90年代的牛车水
4.1. Tourism 21
4.1 “旅游21”
The Tourism Product Development Plan was to run until 1990, but it still influenced policy until the
launch of the Tourism 21 initiative in 1995 (STPB, 1995) after a national tourism planning exercise. The
new strategy seeks to establish Singapore as a Tourism Capital for the 21st century, performing the
roles of outstanding tourist destination, tourism business centre and tourism hub for the region. The six
strategic thrusts are presented of redefining tourism, reformulating the product, developing tourism as
an industry, configuring new tourism space, partnering for success and championing tourism. As part of
the second thrust, 11 thematic zones have been identified, in accordance with the urban design plans
laid out by the URA in its Development Guide Plans, to impose a unifying character on existing products.
Ethnic Singapore is one such zone and Chinatown listed as an ‘opportunity area' alongside Little India,
Geylang Serai and Katong/Joo Chiat (the other traditional ethnic areas) with a ‘story' of ‘a celebration of
diversity'. The strategy document describes the need to formulate an ‘Experience Guide Plan' which will
set out what kind of experience the visitor should have and an appropriate plan for the area. In the case


[READING EDITION PAGE 8]
8
of Chinatown, its signifi- cance ‘can be enhanced if its various streets, buildings and parks were further
integrated into a single themed development. This will ensure that the development of separate
activities within the area is coordinated, both in terms of timing and visual impact' (p. 28). Improved
‘hardware' will include an interpretative centre, better lighting and information boards while ‘software'
involves cultural trails, specialist guides and events and activities. The purpose is to give visitors more
things to see and do in Singapore, encourage a longer length of stay and provide them with strong
reasons to return * thereby maximising tourist expenditure. Progress towards implementation of the
strategy was interrupted by the onset of the Asian financial crisis in 1997 which had an adverse effect
on visitor arrivals throughout the region with Singapore experiencing a fall in numbers for the first time
in over a decade, of as much as 20 per cent in the opening months of 1998 (STB, 1998a). The goal of 10
million arrivals by the year 2000 was thus postponed for another five years. However, the downturn
drew attention to the importance of tourism to Singapore's economy and government still allocates it a
high priority, continuing to pursue the objectives set out in Tourism 21.
《旅游产品发展计划》原定实施至1990年，但直到1995年在全国旅游规划后推出“旅游21”（Tourism
21）之前，它仍在影响政策（STPB, 1995）。新战略希望把新加坡建设成21世纪的“旅游之都”，同时
承担卓越旅游目的地、旅游商业中心和区域旅游枢纽三种角色。战略提出六个重点：重新定义旅游、重
新塑造旅游产品、把旅游发展为产业、配置新的旅游空间、通过合作取得成功，以及积极倡导旅游。作
为第二项重点的一部分，政府依据市区重建局《发展指导规划》中的城市设计方案划定11个主题区，以
为现有旅游产品赋予统一特色。“族裔新加坡”是其中之一；牛车水与小印度、芽笼士乃及加东／如切
等传统族裔地区一道，被列为“机会地区”，其“故事”主题是“庆祝多样性”。战略文件提出要制定
“体验指导规划”，明确游客应该获得何种体验，并为地区制定相应计划。对牛车水而言，如果把不同
街道、建筑和公园进一步整合为一个统一主题的发展项目，其意义就能得到增强；这样可协调区内各项
活动的开发时间与视觉效果（p.28）。“硬件”改善包括阐释中心、更好的照明和信息牌，“软件”则
包括文化步道、专业导游以及各类活动与事件。目标是给游客更多可看、可做的内容，延长停留时间，
并提供再次到访的强烈理由，从而最大化旅游消费。1997年亚洲金融危机爆发，整个地区游客人数受到
影响，新加坡在十多年内首次出现下降，1998年最初几个月的降幅高达20%（STB, 1998a），战略实施
因此受挫。原定2000年实现一千万游客的目标也被推迟五年。不过，经济低迷反而凸显旅游业对新加坡
经济的重要性，政府仍给予其高度优先地位，并继续推进“旅游21”的目标。
4.2. Restoring life to Chinatown
4.2 让牛车水重新“活起来”
Even before Tourism 21 and since 1989, the URA has attempted to restore street life and a sense of
bustling community to Chinatown, working with the Singapore Tourist Promotion Board (which changed
its name to the Singapore Tourism Board * STB * in 1997) in an effort to maintain a balance between
preserving the old and meeting the demands of the present. Infrastructure has been improved, in
association with the Land Transport Authority, and new substations now allow the shophouses to be
fully air conditioned. Extensive restoration of many of the traditional Chinese Baroque style buildings
has been completed and landscaping carried out. Work has started on implementing a pedestrian
network masterplan which ties in with a project to create bazaartype malls in some streets. According
to the Head of Conservation at the URA, this is ‘part of the Government's overall vision to revitalise
Chinatown by developing a pedestrian-friendly environment that will provide the people better access
to shops and facilities, and thereby encouraging a more active streetlife' (Lee, 1997). These measures
are also aimed at easing the traffic problems in places where the narrow streets are congested by the
large numbers of tour coaches. The URA has laid down guidelines encouraging street level shops in the
core area, and stated that such shops should be retailers selling traditional Chinese products and
services or food outlets rather than offices. The National Arts Council has acquired some units to be
given to various literary and performing arts groups including those specialising in Beijing opera,
Chinese classical music and Mandarin or bilingual theatre at a heavily subsidised rent under its Arts
Housing Scheme. As an example of a joint public-private sector partnership in conservation which seeks
to achieve the equilibrium between old and new previously referred to, the URA awarded a tender to
develop one of the parcels of land in Chinatown into Far East Square. Six blocks have been devoted to

conservation with 61 shophouses restored at the developer's expense and the seventh to be an
ultra-modern glass building housing commercial premises. The developer has also funded the
restoration of Singapore's oldest known temple, Fuk Tak Chi, into a street museum with the National
Heritage Board acting as a consultant and incorporated a new Cultural Pavilion into the scheme to be
used for cultural performances and arts exhibitions. Both of these sites will feature in a Heritage Trail
conceptualised by the National Heritage Board which includes the Chor Eng Institute, one of the oldest
free Chinese schools. Former shop tenants around the area have also been invited to move back. The
project is now approaching completion. Whether this particular development will succeed in its
intentions of transforming the area and blending the old and new in an effective and stimulating way
remains to be seen. One problem might be the high rental costs and the list of food and beverage
operators which includes names like Spinelli, Canadian Muffin Co and Kentucky Fried Chicken suggests
established and international chains might dominate. The old Chor Eng Institute is to become a
restaurant and discotheque with others offering Mediterranean, French and Italian cuisine. It is
anticipated that retail outlets will sell books and antiques, amongst other goods, and some will be
devoted to services like banking (Chin, 1998). Such activities and resultant atmosphere would appear to
exclude traditional hawkers and street life with West predominating over East rather than the exciting
combination implicit in the current Tourist Board slogan of Singapore: New Asia which is discussed more
fully later.

旅游局的目标，是把到访牛车水的游客比例从当时占全部入境游客的47%-70%进一步提高，并让他们在

，本地居民同样会从这些改变中受益：开发商被鼓励提出住宅开发方案，同时为新企业进入、现有企业
扩张或多元化创造机会。牛车水将因丰富的景点和活动而成为“人人都愿意停留”的地方。

4.4 “Singapore New Asia”品牌
One aim guiding the authorities in their efforts to develop Chinatown has been the desire to combine
old and new, as well as East and West, in a harmonious way to satisfy the demands of the tourism
industry as well as meet the needs of residents. This is reflected in the STB slogan of Singapore New
Asia which it has employed as a brand since 1996, hoping to convey the exciting mix that exists in
Singapore, ‘a city with its head in the future and its soul in the past'. Thus, the latest in modern
technology co-exists with traditional values and customs in a young nation which looks ahead but does
not forget its heritage; fusion is the key word often employed to describe the intended end result. The
effectiveness of such a branding exercise might be questioned with a degree of confusion amongst both
residents and visitors about what the brand name actually signifies. The weaknesses of the concept are
perhaps illustrated by Far East Square with its uneasy combination of the past and present and the site
in Chinatown where a pub called Pop Cat painted bright blue and decorated with large yellow flowers
stands close to traditional Chinese medicine halls, creating what some might perceive as a sense of
incongruity and discord. The uncertainty was recognised by the STB at a recent conference where
proposals were presented for a programme of popular education in which all Singaporeans were to be
encouraged to ‘live the values of the brand' (STB, 1998b). It could be argued that it is the marketeers
who are confused and the brand should reflect contemporary life rather than dictate it, an argument
which suggests the dangers inherent in place branding, marketing and commodification discussed by
Teo and Yeoh (1997) within the context of Singapore.
在政府发展牛车水的努力中，一个重要目标是把“旧与新”以及“东方与西方”以和谐方式结合起来，
既满足旅游业要求，也回应居民需要。这体现在新加坡旅游局自1996年起使用的品牌口号“Singapore
New Asia（新加坡：新亚洲）”。该品牌希望传达新加坡令人兴奋的混合特征——“一座头脑面向未来
、灵魂属于过去的城市”。因此，最现代的技术与传统价值和习俗并存，一个年轻国家向前看，却不忘
自身遗产；“融合”成为描述理想结果的关键词。不过，这种品牌塑造究竟有多有效值得质疑，因为居
民和游客对品牌名称究竟意味着什么都存在一定困惑。远东广场也许体现了这一概念的弱点：过去与现
在的组合显得并不自然。在牛车水某处，一家名为 Pop Cat 的酒吧被涂成亮蓝色并装饰巨大的黄色花
朵，旁边却是传统中药行，一些人因此会感到不协调甚至冲突。旅游局在一次会议上也承认这种不确定
性，并提出面向公众的教育计划，鼓励所有新加坡人“活出品牌的价值”（STB, 1998b）。也可以反过
来说，真正困惑的是市场营销者：品牌应反映当代生活，而不是规定当代生活应该是什么样。这一论点
揭示了 Teo 与 Yeoh（1997）在新加坡语境中讨论的地方品牌化、营销和商品化所包含的风险。
Matters of design also arise and the need for harmonious new development and sympathetic
restoration, employing appropriate colours and materials. The restored shophouses in Chinatown are
often criticised for the very bright paints used externally which create an impression of newness and of
a stage set, contrasting markedly with the dark and sombre hues of the older properties and their worn
appearance. Modern signs and other items like air-conditioning units can also add a discordant element.
设计问题也随之出现：新的开发需要与环境协调，修复也应具有同理心，并使用恰当的颜色和材料。牛
车水修复后的店屋经常因外墙颜色过于鲜艳而受到批评，因为它们给人一种“全新”甚至舞台布景般的
印象，与旧物业暗沉的色调和磨损痕迹形成强烈对比。现代招牌以及空调机组等设备也可能成为不协调
的视觉元素。

interference, regulation and management. Broad guidelines and incentives could be introduced if
necessary, and local people must be consulted about their feelings and opinions before any
fundamental changes are made. Such sentiments were echoed by a series of letters to the press

residents. Several also pointed to the irony of a policy to bring back to the streets the life, colour, sights

time and Chinatown's history has been one of constant and rapid change, the concern remains that
these changes and new identity are being forced upon it by external agencies to create a manufactured
sense of place. The image of a theme park is often invoked by critics with the Chinatown of the future
described as such an attraction in which locals have no place, except as paid employees. Chinatown as
a community of residents and workers will thus cease to exist, and there is a strong feeling that the
people who make up this community have not as yet been sufficiently consulted about their views.

5. Conclusions
5. 结论
A study of Chinatown and its development raises interesting and topical issues relating to the search for
authenticity in the conservation of heritage and its presentation as a tourist attraction, the role of local
people in the planning process and the applicability of the life cycle model. It illustrates the conflicting
interests at work, the need to achieve some kind of balance amongst them and the difficulties of
securing this. The history of Singapore, its unique society of mixed races and particular political culture
all add to the complexity of the case and introduce further tensions and layers of meaning. Whilst
recognising that each Chinatown and similar historic sites will have a distinctive history, set of current
concerns and future development patterns, it is possible to draw some general lessons from the case
and these are discussed below.
研究牛车水及其发展，会引出一系列当下仍十分重要的问题：在遗产保护并把遗产展示为旅游吸引物时
如何追求真实性；地方居民在规划过程中应发挥什么作用；旅游目的地生命周期模型是否适用。这个案
例展示了多种相互冲突的利益、在它们之间取得某种平衡的必要性，以及实现这种平衡的困难。新加坡
的历史、独特的多种族社会以及特殊政治文化，又进一步增加了案例的复杂性，使其产生更多张力和意
义层次。虽然每一个唐人街或类似历史地点都有自己的历史、当前问题和未来发展轨迹，但仍可以从牛
车水案例中提炼若干一般性经验，以下部分即对此展开讨论。
5.1. Local participation
5.1 地方参与
Since Murphy (1985) stressed the importance of greater involvement of the community in tourism
development, this has become a widely accepted aim amongst planners with destination residents
‘being seen increasingly as the nucleus of the tourism product' (Simmons, 1994, p. 98). Pearce,
Moscardo and Ross (1996) write of the need for a ‘more sophisticated understanding of how
communities react to the burgeoning phenomenon of tourism' (p. 1) in the pursuit of improved
long-term planning and discuss the value of a social representations approach. The debate has since
been extended to incorporate stakeholder theories and arguments about the need to ensure that all
local stakeholders contribute to planning policy making in the interests of sustainability (Sautter &
Leisen, 1999). While accepting the benefits and desirability of this participation, its practicality and
viability has also been questioned and Taylor (1995) describes the perverseness that ‘in approaching
community members to become participants in both the tourism product and its planning, residents are
encouraged to become part of the problem and then given the opportunity to become part of the
solution' (p. 488). There are also difficulties of establishing appropriate and workable mechanisms for
consultation and involvement and evaluating its effectiveness, especially in the developing world and


[READING EDITION PAGE 14]
14
Timothy (1999) lists cultural and political traditions, poor economic conditions, lack of expertise and
lack of understanding by residents as constraints in the case of the Indonesian destination of
Yogyakarta. Many states may also choose to set limits to the extent of public debate and exercise
control over the outcome, either openly or more covertly. However, this problem is also evident in the
countries of the developed world as Bramwell and Sharman (1999) reveal in their account of
stakeholder collaboration in a UK case study where ‘unequal power relations remained among the
stakeholders with the distribution of power weighted towards the authorities rather than the residents'
(p. 412). Timothy also presents a model of participatory tourism planning which includes public
consultation and locally defined goals, input from other stakeholders, involvement of locals in the
benefits of tourism and the education of residents. While there is some evidence of such community
participation in the decision making process in Singapore, it appears to have been arranged at a late
stage in response to popular concern and adverse publicity. Greater efforts are now being made to
consult, involve and educate locals and the recent STB announcement about holding a series of
dialogue sessions indicates further moves in this direction. Conditions in Chinatown thus suggest the
existence of a gap between planning theory and practice, as well as the necessity of local involvement
if tourism development is to succeed in the long term. Ideally, it is not only the inhabitants of Chinatown
who should have a say, but also the population of Singapore as a whole who represent important
stakeholders given the national significance of the site. Teo writing independently (1994) and with
Huang (1995) has described the absence of local participation in other tourism projects in Singapore
and the subsequent alienation and exclusion of the resident population from what become principally
tourism venues. Whenever, and wherever this happens, authenticity and meaning are lost, possibly
forever.
自 Murphy（1985）强调旅游发展应更充分让社区参与以来，这已成为规划者广泛接受的目标，目的地
居民也越来越被视为“旅游产品的核心”（Simmons, 1994, p.98）。Pearce、Moscardo 与 Ross（199
6）指出，为了改进长期规划，需要“更深入理解社区如何回应迅速增长的旅游现象”（p.1），并讨论
社会表征方法的价值。此后讨论进一步纳入利益相关者理论，强调为了实现可持续发展，应确保所有地
方利益相关者都能参与规划政策制定（Sautter & Leisen, 1999）。尽管社区参与的好处与必要性得到
承认，其实际可行性仍受到质疑。Taylor（1995）指出其中的悖论：当社区成员被邀请参与旅游产品及
其规划时，“居民先被鼓励成为问题的一部分，然后又被给予成为解决方案一部分的机会”（p.488）
。此外，如何建立合适而可操作的咨询与参与机制、以及如何评估其成效，也存在困难，发展中国家尤
其如此。Timothy（1999）研究印度尼西亚日惹时列出文化与政治传统、经济条件不佳、专业能力不足
以及居民理解不足等制约。许多国家也可能主动限制公共讨论范围，并以公开或更隐蔽方式控制最终结
果。不过，这并非只存在于发展中国家。Bramwell 与 Sharman（1999）对英国案例中利益相关者合作
的研究同样发现，“利益相关者之间仍存在不平等的权力关系，权力分配偏向政府机构而不是居民”（
p.412）。Timothy 还提出参与式旅游规划模型，其中包括公众咨询和由地方确定目标、其他利益相关
者投入、本地居民分享旅游收益以及居民教育等要素。在新加坡的决策过程中虽然可以看到一定程度的
社区参与，但这种参与似乎是在公众担忧和负面媒体报道出现后才较晚安排的。目前政府正在加强咨询
、参与和居民教育，新加坡旅游局最近宣布举行一系列对话会，也表明这一方向有所推进。牛车水的情
况因此显示出规划理论与实践之间存在差距，也显示旅游发展若要长期成功，地方参与具有必要性。理
想情况下，不仅牛车水居民应该拥有发言权；鉴于该地点具有全国意义，新加坡整体人口同样是重要利
益相关者。Teo 单独（1994）以及与 Huang 合作（1995）的研究都描述了新加坡其他旅游项目中地方
参与缺失，以及居民随后如何被疏离、排除在逐渐主要服务游客的场所之外。无论何时、何地出现这种
情况，真实性和意义都可能丧失，而且也许是永久性的。

5.2. Authenticity and recreating the past
5.2 真实性与过去的再创造
The case also illustrates some of the difficulties associated with the concept of authenticity in the tourist
experience, suggested by MacCannell (1976) and subsequently explored in a series of studies (Cohen,
1979; Pearce & Moscardo, 1986; Selwyn, 1996). With the emergence of an extensive literature on the


[READING EDITION PAGE 15]
15
topic, the subject has acquired a complexity and ambiguity so that satisfactory definitions appear
elusive * especially when postmodernist discussions are considered (Eco, 1986). A recent article by
Wang (1999) provides an excellent summary of the alternative ways of assessing authenticity and
offers a three-fold typology. Objective authenticity refers to that of originals, constructive authenticity is
that which is projected onto objects or experiences by the tourists themselves or tourist industry and
existential authenticity is that which arises from tourist activity and participation. However, it is not just
the tourists and the industry, but also the state which determines forms of authenticity (Bruner, 1994)
with local views often overlooked in this process. Commentators suggest that tourists claim to search
for authentic objects, sites and experiences, but often accept as authentic what might be dismissed as
fake or bogus by experts and intellectuals; the authenticity they seek is thus symbolic rather than real.
Objective authenticity is increasingly uncommon, being replaced by its constructive and existential
forms. In some ways, reality has ceased to exist as we inhabit a postmodern world of simulacra which is
well suited to a tourism industry that deals in images and idealised representations. Tourist
appreciation and understanding of authenticity is a question that merits further study as does the
approach of attractions which claim to be offering it. While accepting that those involved cannot be
expected to depict a site's past in all its squalor, they do have certain responsibilities with regard to
giving visitors an accurate and meaningful sense of the history of a place and its contemporary realities.
It is important for public and private sector providers to try to understand and meet the needs of both
residents and visitors where possible whilst also striving for honesty and integrity of presentation and
interpretation so that ambiguity about the type of authenticity on offer is reduced. There is a danger
that presentation becomes a selective process which reflects the interests of those making the
selection (Barthel, 1989) rather than the local population or indeed the tourists themselves, and that
authenticity is manipulated accordingly. Within the context of Chinatown, part of the past that the
authorities wish to see return and sometimes promise to the tourists is a lively street life, usually
described as ‘vibrant', but the original atmosphere obviously cannot be recovered. The former hustle
and bustle on the streets was a result of the dense population and overcrowded conditions which forced
people out on to the footways and roads for leisure and commerce with a bazaar economy of day and
night markets. Such trading disappeared as a result of the government's housing and urban renewal
policies, and the establishment of a formal economy and planned city displaced the ‘itinerant hawkers,
pedlars and roving traders' (Savage, 1992, p. 19). As well as traders, traditional entertainments and
activities like glove puppet shows and story-telling have also died out, or are in the process of doing so,
as described by former practitioners in a recent oral history project (Lo-Ang & Chua, 1992). While much
of the physical landscape is capable of being restored and has acquired a degree of constructed
authenticity, ways of living and street life cannot be so easily represented and it is unlikely that many
residents or visitors would want to return to the harsh conditions of Chinatown and other historic sites
at the turn of the century. The problem then is determining what and whose authenticity is presented to
the visitor. As the authorities and tourism industry seek to create an atmosphere and environment
which evokes the past and reintroduce traditional activities in modified forms, Chinatown might be seen
as an attempt to devise and present a form of selective and possibly distorted authenticity which avoids
areas of contention and controversy. The emphasis is on making the place an appealing one for visitors
who are encouraged to spend their time and money there. There is the additional challenge of the fact
that those traditional traders who have survived are faced with high rental costs of restored properties
and increases of 100 to 200 per cent on each occasion. For example, the rent for an 800 sq ft corner
shop unit rose from S$80 a month to S$5000 in 1998 for the retailer after the shophouse was renovated
(Li, 1998). These charges make it difficult for the small scale businesses to survive with their places
being taken by fast food outlets, expensive restaurants and smart offices. This objective authenticity of
the present may not always match that sought by the tourist who may also be dissatisfied with the
contrived versions of the past which lies uneasily alongside. Additional research into the actual
responses of visitors to Chinatown and their experiences there is necessary, however, to test such
observations while the attitudes of residents and the formulation of those attitudes also demands
further study.

1986；Selwyn, 1996）。随着相关文献大量出现，这一主题变得十分复杂和含糊，尤其加入后现代主义

讨论后，很难找到令人满意的定义（Eco, 1986）。Wang（1999）的一篇文章很好总结了评估真实性的
不同方式，并提出三分法：客观真实性指原物本身的真实性；建构真实性指游客自己或旅游业投射到物

和旅游业，国家也参与其中（Bruner, 1994），而地方观点经常在此过程中被忽视。研究者指出，游客
常声称自己追求真实的物件、地点与体验，却又会把专家或知识分子认为虚假、伪造的东西视为真实；
因此，他们寻求的往往是象征性而非“真正”的真实性。客观真实性日益少见，逐渐被建构真实性和存
在真实性取代。从某种意义上说，在由拟像构成的后现代世界中，“现实”本身似乎已经消失，而这恰
好适合一个依赖形象和理想化表征运作的旅游业。游客如何理解和评价真实性仍值得进一步研究，那些
声称自己提供“真实体验”的景点采取何种方法也同样需要考察。即使不能要求展示者把一个地点过去
所有肮脏与困苦都重新呈现出来，他们仍有责任让游客获得准确而有意义的历史理解，并认识到当地当
代现实。公共和私人部门的展示者应尽可能理解并同时满足居民和游客需求，并在展示与阐释上追求诚
实和完整，从而减少“所提供的真实性究竟是什么”这一问题的模糊性。一个危险在于，展示可能成为
由选择者利益主导的选择性过程（Barthel, 1989），而不是反映当地居民甚至游客自身的需要，真实
性也会随之被操纵。就牛车水而言，政府希望重新带回、也常向游客承诺的一部分“过去”，是充满活
力的街头生活，但原有氛围显然不可能恢复。过去街上的拥挤与喧闹来自人口高度密集和居住条件过度
拥挤，人们不得不到人行道和道路上休闲、交易，由此形成昼夜市集构成的巴刹经济。随着政府住房与
城市更新政策推进，这类贸易逐渐消失；正式经济和规划城市的建立，也取代了“流动小贩、摊贩和走
街串巷的商人”（Savage, 1992, p.19）。除商人外，布袋戏、讲古等传统娱乐和活动也已经消失或正
在消失，最近的口述历史项目记录了前从业者的回忆（Lo-Ang & Chua, 1992）。大量物质景观可以修
复，也已获得某种建构出来的真实性；但生活方式和街头生活并不能如此轻易重现，而且今天无论居民
还是游客，很可能都不愿回到世纪之交牛车水和其他历史街区的艰苦条件。问题因此变成：究竟应该向
游客呈现什么真实性，以及谁的真实性？当政府和旅游业试图创造一种唤起过去的氛围和环境，并以改
造后的形式重新引入传统活动时，牛车水可能是在设计并展示一种经过选择、甚至有所扭曲的真实性，
主动避开争议与冲突。重点是把这里打造得对游客有吸引力，鼓励他们停留和消费。与此同时，那些幸
存下来的传统商户还面临修复后物业的高租金，每次加租可达100%-200%。例如，一间800平方英尺的角
落店铺修复后，零售商租金从每月80新元上涨到1998年的5,000新元（Li, 1998）。如此成本使小规模
商户难以生存，它们的位置被快餐店、昂贵餐厅和高档办公室取代。当下这种“客观真实性”未必符合
游客想要寻找的真实性，而游客也可能对旁边那些人为设计的过去感到不满。要验证这些判断，还需要
进一步研究游客对牛车水的真实反应和体验，同时也需更深入了解居民态度及其形成过程。

5.3. Product life cycles
5.3 产品生命周期
The life cycle model is commonly used to analyse the evolution of tourism destinations and several
versions have been proposed since the 1960s (Alex Tooman, 1997), although Butler's (1980) remains
perhaps the most influential. He described the stages of the life cycle as being those of exploration,
involvement, development, consolidation, stagnation, and decline or rejuvenation. The concept is not
without its critics, however, who claim that it can only be a guide for planners and marketers (Cooper,
1994) while others argue that it has lost any value it once possessed. Da Conceicao and Roque Aquas
(1997) summarise the limitations as ‘the absence of a prescriptive model that can be widely used and
unanimously accepted; the difficulty in determining to which stage products and services belong; the
lack of data to support the theory; and the concept's subjective nature' (p. 14). It is not the purpose of
this paper to evaluate the product life cycle in detail, the subject already of some debate in the
academic press (Singh, 1998), but it can be concluded that Singapore's Chinatown does conform to the
model in many respects in that it appears to have followed the pattern from early discovery and
exploration to maturity in its history as a tourist attraction. It is also a concept employed by the tourism
authorities who frequently make use of terms such as revitalisation and rejuvenation to describe their
policies. Present developments in Singapore's Chinatown can be seen as a recognition by the tourism
authorities that the site has arrived at the stage of stagnation and possible decline and that the

17
proposed changes are necessary to begin a process of rejuvenation. However, whilst the life cycle
model appears to be a useful instrument to conceptualise a destination's evolution, the question of
whether such a complex entity as Chinatown and its history can be treated like a consumer product
raises some doubts about the appropriateness of its application to places which are also communities.
As such, these sites undergo continual change and this constant development may give rise to shifts in
their identity, both in substance and perception, as tourist destinations. The life cycle model is perhaps
too rigid and inflexible to capture and explain these dynamics. Chinatown thus represents the
challenges facing both conservation agencies and those responsible for present- ing and promoting the
heritage which is conserved to visitors. It provides an illustration of the possibilities and pitfalls of both
processes and offers some interesting insights into the debate about participatory planning and the
search for authenticity, as well as the appropriateness of the life cycle model. The ability to meet these
challenges effectively in Singapore remains debatable with evidence of the benefits of conservation
initiatives and their limitations, as well as both successful and inappropriate tourism development and
moves towards greater community participation. Questions of whose heritage is being preserved, in
what manner and for what purpose need to be addressed, however, to ensure that the Chinatown which
emerges in the new millenium will still be a living and working community that attracts visitors for itself.
Chinatown and similar sites should not be allowed to become stages for the telling of stories devised by
the tourism industry to maximise the sale of its products and sanctioned by government in pursuit of
wider objectives; in this way, locals are denied their rightful opportunity to participate and the visitor
experience is devalued.
生命周期模型常用于分析旅游目的地的演变，自20世纪60年代以来已有多个版本（Alex Tooman,
1997），其中 Butler（1980）的模型可能最具影响力。他把生命周期划分为探索、参与、发展、巩固
、停滞，以及衰退或复兴等阶段。不过，这一概念一直受到批评。有学者认为它至多只能为规划者和市
场营销者提供指导（Cooper, 1994），也有人认为它已经失去原有价值。Da Conceicao 与 Roque Aqua
s（1997）把其局限概括为：“缺乏一个可被广泛使用和一致接受的规范性模型；难以判断产品与服务
属于哪个阶段；缺乏支持理论的数据；以及概念本身具有主观性”（p.14）。本文无意详细评价产品生
命周期——这一问题学界已有讨论（Singh, 1998）——但可以说，新加坡牛车水在许多方面确实符合
该模型：作为旅游景点，它似乎经历了从早期发现、探索到成熟的过程。旅游主管部门也使用这种概念
，经常以“复兴”“再生”等词描述政策。当前牛车水的发展，可以理解为旅游部门认为该地点已进入
停滞并可能衰退的阶段，因此需要通过规划中的改变启动“复兴”。然而，虽然生命周期模型有助于概
念化目的地演变，但牛车水这样复杂的实体同时也是一个社区；把它及其历史像消费产品一样处理，会
让人怀疑这一模型是否真正适用。这样的地方持续变化，而不断发展又会使它们作为旅游目的地的身份
在实际内容和社会认知上发生变化。生命周期模型也许过于僵化，难以捕捉和解释这种动态。牛车水由
此集中体现了遗产保护机构以及负责向游客展示、推广受保护遗产者所面对的挑战。这个案例说明两种
过程都存在可能性和陷阱，并为参与式规划、真实性追求以及生命周期模型是否恰当等争论提供了有意
义的观察。在新加坡，能否有效应对这些挑战仍存在争议：一方面可看到保护举措带来的收益及其局限
；另一方面既有成功也有不合适的旅游开发，同时社区参与也有所增加。真正必须回答的问题是：保护
的是谁的遗产？以何种方式保护？为了什么目的？只有这样，才能确保进入新千年后的牛车水仍是一个
真实有人生活、有人工作的社区，并因其自身特质吸引游客。牛车水以及类似地点不应变成舞台，用来
上演旅游业为了最大化产品销售而设计、并得到政府为追求更广泛目标而认可的故事。否则，当地人会
被剥夺应有的参与机会，游客体验本身也会因此贬值。
