# Causes and Effects of Loneliness among University Students

Loneliness among university students can arise from disrupted friendships and a lack of meaningful connections. Students entering a new university may lose regular contact with familiar people while struggling to build new relationships. For example, eating alone or visiting a doctor without support may feel particularly lonely when students want someone to accompany them. Academic pressure may make these situations harder to manage. Ellard et al. (2023) note that university students often face the combined challenges of leaving home and managing academic, financial and social pressures. However, loneliness can also occur when other people are present. Quinn (2025) emphasises that students need a genuine sense of belonging, rather than simply fitting in. Thus, a student at a party may still feel lonely if conversations remain superficial or they feel misunderstood. Overall, loneliness can develop when students lack companionship or feel that their relationships do not provide enough emotional support.

Loneliness can affect university students' emotional well-being, sleep and everyday functioning. Ellard et al. (2023) report that loneliness is associated with anxiety, stress and depression among students, as well as sleep problems and lower life satisfaction. These findings suggest that loneliness may involve more than a temporary feeling of sadness. For example, students who feel disconnected may find it difficult to enjoy campus life, even when they attend classes and activities regularly. Sleep problems may also leave them tired the next day, potentially making it harder to concentrate in lectures and complete assignments. As these difficulties accumulate, ordinary academic demands may feel more stressful. Feeling overwhelmed could then make it harder to manage both study and social commitments. Although these associations do not establish that loneliness causes every difficulty, they highlight its potential consequences. Overall, loneliness may undermine students' emotional health and their ability to manage daily university life.
