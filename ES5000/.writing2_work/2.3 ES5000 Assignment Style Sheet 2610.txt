ASSIGNMENT STYLE SHEET 
 
Please follow these guidelines to format all your ES5000 assignments before submission. See 
the example on the next page. 
 
The Basics ▪ Leave at least a 1" (2.54 cm) margin on all sides of your A4 size paper 
(top, bottom, left and right). 
▪ For documents with multiple pages, use page numbers. 
 
Page 
Heading 
▪ Put your name, ES5000 tutorial group number and the date on separate 
lines at the top left-hand corner. 
▪ Use single line spacing for the heading. 
 
Body Text ▪ Give each assignment a descriptive title, centred, on its own line. 
▪ To distinguish separate paragraphs, skip a line between paragraphs. 
▪ Use 1.5 line spacing. Align text to the left (not full justification). 
▪ Use a 12-point font for body text.  
▪ Count the words and put the word count after the final paragraph (e.g., 
W=400) 
 
Quotations ▪ For short quotes (of no more than 40 words), use “quotation marks” to 
identify the quoted text. 
▪ For long quotes (of 40 words or more), start the quotation on a separate 
line and indent every line of the quoted material 10 spaces (or two 
tabs). 
 
Spacing and 
punctuation 
▪ Put one space after commas, full stops, colons and semi-colons. Do not 
use spaces before punctuation marks. 
▪ Put no space between the numeral and the percent sign or dollar sign 
(for example, 87%, $400, etc.). 
▪ Put a comma but no space in large numbers to indicate thousands, 
millions, etc. (for example, 18,000,000). 
 
Numbers ▪ Spell numbers less than ten. Use figures for numbers 10 and over. 
▪ Spell out numbers that are the first word of a sentence. 
▪ For numbers less than 1, precede the decimal point with a zero (for 
example, 0.36). 
 
References 
and 
Declaration 
▪ Cite sources in the text using APA citation style. 
▪ At the end of the assignment (after the References) copy and paste the 
AI Declaration given in Canvas. 
▪ Use single line spacing for the reference list and AI declaration. 
 
File Names ▪ Name your assignment files with the “tutorial group number_your 
name_assignment”. 
▪ Example: G10_Teo Wei Jie_p-s essay

Grammar Analysis of a Computer Visualization Text  
 
In Detecting and Reading Text in Natural Scenes, Chen and Yuille (2004) described their 
study of a new algorithm designed to process the digitized information from a road-sign 
reading device. In their study, the authors often used comparison and contrast to present the 
efficiency and advantages of the algorithm. Two examples of adjectives in comparative 
degree are found in the conclusion. Firstly, “The performance we report in this paper is 
better than those reported in Lucas et al., but the datasets are different” (p. 273). Secondly, 
“Also there are important differences between text and face stimuli because the spatial 
variation per pixel of text images is far greater than for faces” (p. 273). 
 
Furthermore, the authors also used adverbs in mid position. For instance, the following 
examples come from the discussion section: 
Such tests are computationally cheap to implement because they involved

20,000,000 image windows, the total number of false positives was just over
